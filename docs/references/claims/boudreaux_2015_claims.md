# Claims: Boudreaux 2015 (Jackson Landing, MDAH Archaeological Report 36)

**Full citation**: Boudreaux, Edmond A., III. 2015. *Archaeological Investigations at Jackson Landing: An Early Late Woodland Mound and Earthwork Site in Coastal Mississippi*. Archaeological Report No. 36. Mississippi Department of Archives and History, Jackson. (Contributions by Kandace D. Hollenbach, Kelsey M. Lowe, and Susan L. Scott.)

Source: OCR'd via tesseract/ocrmypdf from `docs/references/pdfs/AR36_Jackson-Landing.pdf` (clean text sidecar: `AR36_Jackson-Landing.ocr.txt`). Page numbers below are the monograph's printed page numbers.

## Site overview (p. 3)
- **Statement**: "The Jackson Landing site is one of the largest and most significant archaeological sites on Mississippi's Gulf coast. The site comprises a 60-acre area that includes a 1.5-m-tall platform mound, a large shell midden, and a semicircular earthen wall that is 460 m long and 3 to 4.5 m tall." Occupations span the last ~4,000 years: minor Late Archaic (ca. 3000-1200 BC), late Late Woodland (AD 700-1200), Mississippi (AD 1200-1550), and an 18th-c. Historic Indian component.
- **Statement**: "Perhaps the most significant episode … occurred during the early Late Woodland period (AD 400-700), when the site was a regional center marked by monumental construction of the site's earthwork and platform mound (Boudreaux 2011a)."
- **Relevance**: The Pearl River coast's first monumental construction postdates the Poverty Point period by ~1,500 years. Used in manuscript §5.1/§6.4 to show the coastal setting was not permanently unsuited to monumentality; it simply did not cross the threshold during the PP window.

## Late Archaic "Pearl River phase" component (p. 9)
- **Statement**: "A Late Archaic component is represented by deposits in two parts of the site. Several radiocarbon dates … suggest an occupation during the Pearl River phase (ca. 3000-1200 BC) of the Late Archaic period (Blitz and Mann 2000; Gagliano 1963:116; Gagliano et al. 1982:39). The type site of this phase is the Cedarland site (22HA506) … approximately 1 km north of Jackson Landing."
- **Statement**: "Similarities between Cedarland and the Late Archaic component at Jackson Landing include the presence at the latter of a pierced Poverty Point object (C. Webb 1982:35) and the absence of pottery (Blitz and Mann 2000:20)."
- **Statement**: An intact basal Late Archaic layer in the Mulatto Bayou shell midden contained a well-preserved hearth (oyster + Rangia shell, charcoal) dated **1780±80 BC (cal 2455-1920 BC, Beta 36062)** and **1700±80 BC (cal 2280-1775 BC, Beta 36064)**; a **perforated Poverty Point object** was found near/in association with the hearth (Giardino and Jones 1996:15-16). Features under the western end of the earthwork gave **1370±80 BC** and **1380±60 BC**, one from a posthole that also contained **a piece of soapstone**, "a raw material frequently found in Late Archaic period contexts (Sassaman 1993)" (Giardino and Jones 1996:30).
- **Relevance**: Documents that the Pearl River coastal sites (Cedarland, and the Late Archaic component at Jackson Landing; cf. Claiborne 22HA501) participated in the Poverty Point-period exchange sphere (Poverty Point objects, soapstone/steatite) without building monuments during the PP period. Independent support for treating the Claiborne/Cedarland coastal pair as the framework's period-specific negative case (§6.4).

## Notes
- Underlying primary sources cited by Boudreaux for the Late Archaic dates and artifacts: Giardino and Jones 1996; Blitz and Mann 2000; C. Webb 1982; Gagliano 1963; Gagliano et al. 1982. The early Late Woodland monumental episode rests on Boudreaux 2011a.
- Claiborne (22HA501) is treated regionally as slightly later (Gulf Formational, ca. 1200-800 BC) than Cedarland; both are pre-monumental coastal shell/earth middens.
