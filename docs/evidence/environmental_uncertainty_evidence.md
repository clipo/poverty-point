# Environmental Uncertainty Evidence

## Key Questions
- What was the climate regime during the Poverty Point period, and how variable was it?
- What drove environmental uncertainty at timescales relevant to human decision-making?
- Does the environmental context support the costly signaling model's prediction that intermediate uncertainty (sigma) favors cooperative signaling?

## Evidence

### From Shuman and Marsicek 2016
- A stepwise transition at ca. 5.5 ka initiated widespread cooling and increases in effective moisture, establishing the climatic regime under which the Poverty Point occupation developed (p. 38)
- Mean regional temperatures were relatively stable from 4.7 to 2.9 ka but with a notable temperature minimum at 3.9-3.5 ka, overlapping with the early Poverty Point period (p. 42)
- A major drought at 4.2-3.9 ka preceded the Poverty Point florescence; drought episodes at 3.6-3.3 ka (~1650-1350 BCE) fall within the Poverty Point period; and a later drought at 3.0-2.4 ka overlaps with its terminal phase (p. 42)
- These drought episodes were "less severe than the 4.7-4.0 ka drought" but had "sufficient severity across records to temporarily decrease the median and mean effective moisture z-scores" (p. 42)
- Temperature and moisture were strongly inversely correlated (r = -0.90) over the last 7 ka: warm periods were dry, cool periods were wet (p. 42)
- Abrupt climate events had magnitudes equal to 25-50% of long-term multi-millennial changes, producing "significant ecological and geomorphic consequences" (p. 48)
- The study region's records are concentrated north of 39 degrees N latitude, requiring cautious extrapolation to Poverty Point at 32.6 degrees N (limitation noted)

### From Salonen et al. 2025
- "Proxy data for eastern North American hydroclimate indicate strong and persistent multi-millennial droughts during the Holocene" (p. 1)
- Mid-Holocene drought was driven not by reduced precipitation but by "temperature-driven increases in evapotranspiration" exceeding precipitation gains (p. 1)
- Neoglacial cooling beginning around 4 ka (2000 BCE) initiated a shift toward wetter conditions; the Poverty Point period (3.7-3.1 ka) occurred during the transition from peak drought to amelioration (p. 4)
- Centennial-scale water balance variations of up to approximately 50 mm were spatially incoherent across regions, meaning different areas experienced drought at different times (p. 6)
- A ~200-year periodicity in moisture variability was identified in the Midwest, possibly linked to solar Suess/de Vries cycles (p. 6)
- The 5.5-5.0 ka wet event slightly precedes the Poverty Point period, suggesting temporarily favorable conditions before the onset of aggregation (p. 6)
- Study regions centered at 42-51 degrees N; Poverty Point at 32.63 degrees N falls south of these clusters, limiting direct applicability (limitation noted)

### From Kaufman et al. 2020
- A global compilation of 1,319 quality-controlled Holocene paleotemperature records from 679 sites; all records include data between 8,500 and 3,500 BP, guaranteeing coverage of the Poverty Point interval (pp. 1, 6)
- All proxy types show a cooling trend from approximately 6,500-5,000 BP through the present, with a global cooling of approximately 0.5 degrees C (pp. 7-8)
- North America has comparatively high site density in the database; the 30-60 degrees N band is the most data-rich (p. 6)
- Median temporal resolution of 164 years means the ~600-year Poverty Point occupation is represented by only a few data points per record (p. 6)
- High-resolution composites capture greater centennial-scale variability than low-resolution composites, indicating that sub-centennial fluctuations were present but undersampled (p. 8)

### From Kidder 2006
- "High-resolution climate data demonstrates an episode of rapid global climate change involving significant alterations in temperature and precipitation in the period ca. 3000-2600 cal B.P." (p. 195)
- Increased precipitation caused by global climate change "led to more floods and to floods of greater magnitude and duration" (p. 196)
- "In the Lower Mississippi River Valley, there was an abrupt alteration in regional landscape stability after circa 3300 cal BP and before 3000 cal BP. Meander belt relocation caused frequent flooding throughout the alluvial valley, resulting in channel switching, extensive crevassing, environmental disruption, and site burial" (p. 196)
- A Bayesian analysis of radiocarbon dates reveals a chronological gap of approximately 400 years (ca. 3150-2700 cal B.P.) between the end of Poverty Point and the beginning of Early Woodland in the lower Mississippi Valley (p. 203)
- "At present there is no unambiguous evidence of temporal continuity from Late Archaic to Early Woodland in any region of Mississippi River basin where there are good data" (p. 212)

## Synthesis

The paleoclimate evidence establishes that the Poverty Point period (ca. 3700-3100 BP / 1700-1100 BCE) occupied a transitional climate phase characterized by three key features. First, the occupation fell within a long-term Neoglacial cooling and moistening trend that followed the end of the Holocene thermal maximum at approximately 5500 BP. Conditions were ameliorating from peak mid-Holocene drought but had not yet reached modern moisture levels. Second, superimposed on this trend were centennial-scale drought episodes that created recurrent environmental perturbations. Shuman and Marsicek (2016) identify drought at 3.6-3.3 ka (~1650-1350 BCE), falling squarely within the period of peak Poverty Point activity. Salonen et al. (2025) document ~200-year periodicities in moisture variability, suggesting that the approximately 600-year Poverty Point occupation would have experienced roughly three such cycles. Third, the Poverty Point system collapsed coincident with a major climate event at approximately 3000-2600 cal BP that dramatically increased flooding and landscape instability throughout the Mississippi River basin.

This environmental pattern maps onto the costly signaling model's predictions in several ways. The intermediate level of uncertainty, severe enough to make cooperation valuable but not so extreme as to make aggregation impossible, matches the theoretical condition where signaling systems become adaptive. The spatial incoherence of centennial drought events, where different regions experienced aridity at different times, would have made inter-regional cooperation and information exchange particularly valuable, increasing the returns to aggregation-based signaling. The mechanism of environmental stress also matters: drought was driven by elevated evapotranspiration rather than reduced precipitation, meaning resource impacts operated through elevated warmth and moisture loss rather than simple aridity.

The terminal event, when meander belt relocation and catastrophic flooding after 3300 cal BP disrupted landscape stability across the entire lower Mississippi Valley, provides a plausible mechanism for system collapse. Environmental uncertainty crossed the threshold where aggregation-based cooperation was no longer viable: exchange networks were severed by flooding, sites were buried, and a 400-year occupational hiatus ensued. This aligns with the model's prediction that when sigma exceeds a critical threshold, the signaling system breaks down.

## Gaps
- No high-resolution paleoclimate records exist from the immediate Poverty Point vicinity (32.63 degrees N, 91.41 degrees W); all reconstructions require extrapolation from records north of 39 degrees N
- The magnitude and timing of centennial drought events at the latitude of Poverty Point remain poorly constrained
- Proxy records have median resolution of 164 years, insufficient for detecting decadal-scale variability that would have been most relevant to year-by-year resource decisions
- Resource-specific impacts of climate variability (effects on nut mast, fish populations, waterfowl migrations) are inferred rather than directly measured
- The relationship between regional climate variability and local resource uncertainty at the Macon Ridge ecotone is not established
- Whether the 3.6-3.3 ka drought episode accelerated or triggered monument construction remains unclear
